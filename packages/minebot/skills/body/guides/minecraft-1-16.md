guide("minecraft-1-16"): what Minecraft: Java Edition has had since 1.16 that the game did
not always have: the large updates up to that version that change how it is played.
(body()["version"] says which version this server runs; later versions add more.)

The difficulty and what a death costs are the server's own settings: as the game is sold a
death drops the inventory where the body died, and the body respawns at its spawn point
with empty hands; a server may keep the inventory instead.

Fighting (since 1.9).
  A weapon recharges after each swing: a sword in 0.6 seconds, an axe in 1 to 1.25
  seconds (wood and stone the slowest), a pickaxe in 0.8, the bare hand in 0.25. A hit
  made before the weapon has recharged does a small part of its damage. An axe does
  more per hit than a sword of the same material and recharges more slowly.
  A sword swung fully recharged by a player who is on the ground and not sprinting
  also hits the mobs beside the target.
  A sword no longer blocks. A shield does: held in the hand or the off hand and raised,
  it stops attacks, arrows and explosions from the front. An axe hit disables it for 5
  seconds.
  The player has an off hand: a second slot that holds an item beside the main hand.
  With food at 20 and saturation left, health comes back fast, 1 point every half
  second, using that saturation.

The End and the dragon (since 1.9).
  Every obsidian pillar has an end crystal on top. On two of the pillars the crystal
  stands in a cage of iron bars.
  A crystal near the dragon heals it. A crystal blows up when anything hits it.
  The dragon lands on the bedrock fountain in the middle from time to time. While it
  sits there arrows bounce off it.
  The dragon breathes and shoots fireballs that leave a cloud on the ground; the cloud
  hurts whoever stands in it.
  The fountain with its bedrock pillar stands from the start; its portal opens when the
  dragon dies. A gateway to the outer islands appears then too. The outer islands have
  end cities, shulkers and the elytra.

Water (since 1.13).
  The player swims: sprinting in water lowers the body flat, moves it fast and lets it
  dive and pass through gaps one block high.
  Drowned are zombies that live under water; some throw tridents.
  Phantoms attack from the sky at night when the player has not slept for three days.
  Oceans have shipwrecks, ruins and buried treasure with chests, and kelp, coral and
  icebergs.
  A magma block under water pulls what is above it down; soul sand under water lifts it
  up. Items float up in water.
  Blocks and items are named by words (oak_log, stone), not by numbers.

Villages and raiders (since 1.14).
  Villagers have a profession from the work block near them and trade by it; they
  sleep in beds and gather at a bell. Villages differ by biome.
  Pillagers shoot crossbows. They stand at outposts and walk in patrols. Killing a
  patrol's captain gives Bad Omen; entering a village with it starts a raid.
  A blast furnace smelts ore and a smoker cooks food twice as fast as a furnace. A
  campfire cooks four pieces of food with no fuel, and hurts whoever stands on it.
  A sweet berry bush hurts and slows whoever walks through it.
  The player fits under a gap one and a half blocks high while sneaking.

Bees (since 1.15).
  Bees attack whoever harms one or takes from their nest with no campfire under it.

The Nether (1.16).
  Biomes: nether wastes, crimson forest, warped forest, soul sand valley, basalt deltas.
  Piglins attack a player who wears no piece of gold armour. A player who breaks a gold
  block, gold ore, nether gold ore, gilded blackstone, a chest, a barrel or a shulker box
  angers every piglin within 16 blocks of it, seen or not, that is not fighting or
  looking at gold; one who opens a chest angers the piglins that see it. An angered
  piglin attacks for 30 seconds, then calms down. Given a gold ingot a piglin looks at it
  for a few seconds and throws back an item: ender pearls 20 times in 423, 4 to 8 of
  them, or obsidian, fire resistance potions, string, iron nuggets, gravel, leather and
  others (a server's data packs may change such tables).
  A blaze killed by the player drops a blaze rod half the time.
  Zombified piglins are the zombie pigmen under a new name: they leave the player alone
  until one is hit.
  Hoglins attack on sight and drop porkchops. Striders walk on lava.
  Bastion remnants hold piglins, gold and chests. Ruined portals stand in the Nether and
  in the Overworld, with a chest that can hold obsidian, flint and steel and gold.
  Blackstone works as cobblestone does for stone tools and a furnace.
  Ancient debris gives netherite, which upgrades diamond gear at a smithing table.
  A respawn anchor, charged with glowstone, sets the place the player comes back to in
  the Nether.
